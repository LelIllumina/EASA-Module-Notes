## Overview

A method of NDT for checking external cracks using penetrant sprays. Not as common as other Methods.

## Applicability

This method is only applicable for external cracks. It cannot detect internal damage.

## Requirements

- Part must be Depainted
- Penetrant Kit:
	- Cleaner/Remover
	- Penetrant
	- Developers
- Pre and Post cleaning.
- For fluorescent Penetrant:
	- UV Light or
	- Ultraviolet Light or
	- Black Light
	- at 1000 micro watts / cm^2
	- visible light should be reduced

## Steps

1. Clean surface with Cleaner/Remover
2. Spray Penetrant on surface
3. Wait for PDT
4. Use rag to clean the top Layer of penetrant
5. Spray Developer
6. Wait for DDT
7. Check Surface for Penetrant

If any Penetrant is now visible after DDT that area has an external crack. The Developer pulled out the penetrant which had seeped into the cracks.

## Pros/Cons

| Pros                          | Cons                                       |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Takes less time               | Requires Depainting                        |
| Material cost is lower        | Penetrant Material is hard to remove/Messy |
| Material is readily available | Only indicates length and not depth        |

## Definitions

- Penetrant Dwell Time (PDT): The amount of time the penetrant needs to rest on the surface of the part. Varies with temperature, Winter season PDT is around 50 minutes whilst Summer season PDT is 30 minutes.
- Developer Dwell Time (DDT): The amount of time the developer needs to rest. Half of PDT.
- Capillary Action: Tiny Pathways are able to move Fluid upwards/Against the force of gravity. (Out of syllabus: Capillary Action occurs due to the attractive forces between the fluid and the capillary tubes. I.e. Surface tension. The opposite of this happens with fluids that dislike "wetting" the surface of the tubes, such as mercury and glass.)
