## What Is EASA?

EASA which stands for the European Aviation Safety Agency, is the European Unions main agency for creating, maintaining, and regulating safety and environmental rules for aviation purposes. Its responsible for rule making, Inspections, Safety certification, approval of designs, data collection, and other duties related to Aviation and Aircraft. EASA does not deal with Civil Aviation and Airport Security.  

The agency's currently located in Cologne, Germany and was established in Brussels, Belgium in 2002 taking over the responsibilities of the JAA[^1] in 2009. Unlike the JAA, EASA works directly under the EU, whereas the JAA has to rely on the member states to cooperate individually

## What Is a Part-66 (Part-145, Part-147)?

"Part-66" refers to Annex-III of the "Commission Regulation (EU) No 1321/2014"[^2]. This is part of EASA's regulations for "Continuing Airworthiness". It dictates the obligations and privileges of an individually responsible for certifying an Aircraft for use after Maintenance and the requirements to gain an AML. It is one of the base requirements to issue a [[#What Is a CRS?|CRS]]. The categories available at ATH are the B1 (Mechanical) and B2 (Avionics) licenses but other categories also exist, as listed:

- A (Minor scheduled tasks)
- B2L (Limited B2 License)
- B3 (Piston-engine airplanes)
- C (Base Maintenance)
- L (Light Aircraft)

Its Syllabus consists of 17 Modules, where each category has its own set of required modules. To pass each module students need to answer MCQs and get a 75% or higher score. The exception is module 7 which requires an Essay in addition.

Alongside Part-66 there also exists a Part-145 which is for the approval of maintenance organizations, and a Part-147 which is for the approval of training organizations and schools. Both of which are related to Part-66 License students.

## What Is a Type Rating?

A type rating is an endorsement on an AML that authorizes the holder to perform and certify maintenance on a specific make and model of an aircraft. It authorizes you to issue a [[#What is a CSR?|Certificates of release to service (CRS)]].

An endorsement of a type-rating on your license requires "Type-Training" or a "Type-Course". This is training on a specific aircraft model and contains 3 (Training) levels. Each level contains information about different parts of the aircraft. An individual needs to pass a level 3 (which includes the previous two levels as well) Type-Course to get a type-rating.

## What Is a CRS?

A Certificate of release to service is a declaration made by an appropriate authorized personnel when the task being signed off has been properly completed. Whenever ordered maintenance or servicing is performed a CRS must be issued for that specific task when it has been verified to be done according to appropriate rules.

It contains info such as the details of the tasks carried out, the date of maintenance, the details of the personnel issuing the CRS, etc.

## What Is Airworthiness?

According to ICAO[^3] Annex 8, the term airworthy means:

> [!quote]
> The status of an aircraft, engine, propeller or part when it conforms to its approved design and is in a condition for safe operation.

Airworthiness is actually 2 parts, "Initial Airworthiness" and "Continuing Airworthiness".

Initial Airworthiness refers to making sure that an aircraft is physically suitable to flying after production, where all the parts are made in accordance to regulations and are tested to be safe under those regulations' limits/requirements. These aircraft's conditions are measured for their whole life cycle rather than for a temporary time period.

Continuing Airworthiness refers to maintaining aircraft to be in a condition it was designed for, under rules and regulations, between flights and after use.

## Aircraft Groups [Link](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/easy-access-rules/95cada/ERULES-1963177438-545)

- Group 1: complex motor-powered aircraft; multi-engine helicopters; other than piston-engine airplanes, with maximum certified operating altitude exceeding FL290; aircraft equipped with fly-by-wire systems; gas airships other than ELA2.
- Group 2: aircraft other than those in Group 1 belonging to the following subgroups:
	- subgroup 2a
		- single turboprop engine airplanes,
		- those turbine-engine airplanes classified by the Agency in this subgroup because of their lower complexity.
- subgroup 2b
		- single turbine engine airplanes,
		- those multiple turbine engine helicopters classified by the Agency in this subgroup because of their lower complexity.
- subgroup 2e
		- single piston engine airplanes,
		- those multiple piston engine helicopters classified by the Agency in this subgroup because of their lower complexity.
- Group 3: piston engine airplanes other than those in Group 1.
- Group 4: sailplanes, powered sailplanes, balloons, and airships, other than those in Group 1.
