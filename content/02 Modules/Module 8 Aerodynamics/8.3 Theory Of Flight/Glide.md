## Overview

If an aircraft's engine stops producing thrust, The aircraft will only have the other 3 forces left acting upon it. It will now start to Glide

## Key Points

- Optimum Glide angle produces the best Glide ratio, i.e. it covers the most distance per unit of descent
- Glide angle is independent of weight and depends on the lift to drag ratio. This is because higher weight also cause higher lift and drag, and total reaction. However, this will require greater speed
- Essentially, the Horizontal component of the Weight Force/Vector is working as thrust to push the aircraft ahead. We need to find the angle optimal for the best glide ratio

## Definitions

Glide Ratio: Unit of vertical distance descended per horizontal distance traveled. For eg, as aircraft covering 10m for every 1m descent has a ratio of 1:10.

Total Reaction: Resultant of [[Lift]] and [[Drag]] forces.

Glide Angle: Angle between the total reaction and Lift. Higher Lift:Drag ratio cause smaller gliding angles, increasing the glide ratio
