## Overview

There are 4 basic forces acting on an aircraft in flight. Its better to have some increased while others should be minimized.

## Key Points

- The four forces:
	- Lift
		- Primarily made by the wings.
		- Acts at right angles to the relative airflow and chord line of the aircraft.
		- At constant Velocity, Lift = Weight
	- Weight
		- Product of mass and Gravitational acceleration.
		- Caused by Gravity
		- CG is usually located along the center near the front of the aircraft. Near the Center of Lift
		- CG Depends on the weight distribution inside the plane
	- Thrust
		- Produced by the engines.
		- Overcomes [[Drag]].
	- Drag
		- Term for air resistance.
		- Points in the direction opposite of motion, i.e. opposite and parallel to the relative airflow.
- The forces move around during flight as circumstances change.
- They may or may not act on the same point of an aircraft.
- Pitching movements:
	- If the CP is behind the CG, this will cause a nose down movement. In terms of moments, the CG acts as a pivot and the CP is a force acting upwards.
	- If the CP is ahead of the CG this will do the opposite. A nose up effect.
	- If the Thrust line is above the CG, nose up.
	- If the Thrust line is below the CG, nose down.
- In cruise flight all these forces should balance out to cause 0 pitching movement.

## Definitions

- Center of Pressure (CP) : An assumed point where all the forces of pressure on an object are averaged on. The CP changes depending on the flow of air.
- Center of Gravity (CG): Assumed point where all the force of gravity is acting upon when viewing an object as a whole.
- Thrust Line: An imaginary line passing through the propeller, perpendicular to its rotation.
