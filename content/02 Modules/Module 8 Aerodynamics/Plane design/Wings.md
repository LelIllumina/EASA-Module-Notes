## Overview

Wing shape, tilts, and mounting positions affects stability and lift in aircraft. This is also called the aircraft's Wing Planform.

## Key Points

### Aspect Ratio

The aspect ratio (AR) of a wing is its Span divided by its area or (average) chord length:

$$
\begin{align*}
AR = \frac{b^2}{S} \quad \text{(for any wing shape)} \\ \\
AR = \frac{b}{c} \quad \text{(for rectangular wings)}
\end{align*}

$$

It determines the length and slenderness of the wingspan when seen from above. A low AR, and thus shorter wings, cause higher [[Induced Drag]] as compared to larger AR wings, but in return have better structural characteristics and maneuverability. Low AR wings are usually on fighters and aircraft that reach supersonic speeds, while High AR is used on High-altitude aircraft thanks to their greater lift. However, Moderate AR wings are the most common.

### Wing Angle/Tilt

The angle created from the wing root to the wingtip against the lateral axis. It affects the stability of the aircraft. A flat wing (No angle) is neutral, whereas a [[Dihedral Wing]] is stabler and an Anhedral Wing is unstable. A Dihedral is tilted so the wingtip is higher than the wing root, while an Anhedral is the opposite with the wingtip lower than the wing root.

The principle at play which increases (Lateral) stability is shown in "[[Dihedral Wing]]". When tilted, the aircraft wing which is lower is closer to being flat and the aircraft wing higher has a steeper angle than at its original position. This causes the air hitting the wings from below (Air that causes Lift/Relative Airflow) to hit the lower wing Perpendicularly and the higher wing at an angle. The perpendicular vector's Y-component (vertical effect) is greater than the same quantity force on the angled wing as it gets divided horizontally as well.

Another way to explain this principle is to say that when the aircraft is tilted, the lower wing presents a larger span of the wing to the relative airflow. This increases lift on that wing pushing it higher and stabilizing the aircraft by returning it to its regular position.

### Wing Mounting Position

Vertically where the wings are attached to the fuselage. As with wing angles, mounting positions affect lateral stability and can be classified in 3 groups, High (Stable), Mid (Neutral) and Low (Unstable). Each category refers to the location of the wing root on the fuselage and changes how the keel/pendulum effect works on the aircraft.

The higher the wing the more "pendulum stability" it has, allowing the aircraft to stay stable without need for a Dihedral wing, or conversely, despite an Anhedral wing. The pendulum effect is created when an aircraft CG is pulled down by gravity and rolls the aircraft like a pendulum. Overall, High-wing stability is produced by the Keel effect, Dihedral effect and pendulum effect together.

Low wing aircraft have higher maneuverability but lower overall lateral stability, making them common on fighter aircraft and such. Meanwhile, cargo planes may have high mounted wings for easier unloading for military freighters, and use Anhedral wings to counter the "over-stability". However, commercial cargo planes use low wing configurations to improve maneuverability by allowing the CG to be higher relative to the wing.

Wing position also affects "Ground effect". Also besides these 3 categories, Shoulder, and Parasol mounted wings exist as well.

### Wing Shapes

A wings shape, i.e. the difference in its chord length from the roots to the tips, has many affects on different aspects of the plane such as lift and drag produced. Some of the shapes commonly found are:

- Rectangular (Constant Chord)
- Tapered
- (Semi-)Elliptical
- Delta
- Other experimental/lesser used shapes

Besides the shape of the wings, they can also be "Swept-back" where the wingtips are swept rearwards to be behind the wing roots (in their own plane). This shape provides better stability and lower drag during transonic speeds, but this comes at the cost of poor handling during a stall and needing better support and structural strength

## Definitions

- Stability: The tendency of the aircraft to return to its original position (after being disturbed)
- Static Stability: The initial tendency for stability
- Dynamic Stability: The tendency for stability over time
- CG: Center of Gravity
- CP: Center of Lift/Pressure; Point representing the sum of all aerodynamic forces
- Relative Airflow: The direction of the air hitting the aircraft

- ---

- Wing Roots: The section where the wing connects to the fuselage.
- Wing Tip: The lateral end of the wing.
- Chord Line: Imaginary line running from the leading edge to the trailing edge of a wing
