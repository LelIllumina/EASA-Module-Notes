## Overview

Density describes how much mass is packed into a given volume of air. It changes with altitude and is directly linked to both [[Pressure]] and [[Temperature|Temperature]].

## Key Points

- Lower altitude has denser air
- Density increases as Pressure increases (Directly Proportional)
- Density decreases as Temperature increases (Inversely Proportional)
- Lower air densities make flight harder
- A standard [[Temperature]] of 4C is used when measuring density
- Density is measured at standard atmospheric [[Pressure]] (1013.25mb)

- ---

- Relative Density (Also called Specific Gravity) is the ratio of a substances density with the datum, i.e. pure water at 4C (Or air for gasses)
- That is to say 1 liter of a fluid with 0.8 RD will weigh 800 Grams because 1 liter of water weighs 1000 grams (a ratio of 0.8:1)
- Substances with RD > 1 Sink and < 1 float due to Archimedes principle
- RD can be measured using a Hydrometer
	- The float inside the hydrometer shows on a scale the RD of the fluid drawn inside the hydrometer
- RD measurements in aviation is useful for battery liquid Electrolytes and aircraft fuel.

## Mass Comparison

The Mass of 1 cubic meter of:

- Water is 1000kg
- Air is 1.204kg

## Definitions

- Density is mass over volume

$$
\text{Density} = \rho = \frac{m}{v}
$$

- Relative density is Density of a substance over density of water/air

  $$

\text{Relative Density/Specific Gravity} = \frac{\text{Density of Substance}}{\text{Density of Water/Air}}

$$
